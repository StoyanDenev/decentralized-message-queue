# Per-block K-of-K committee selection vs. the epoch-anchored seed — analysis, verdict, minimal design, review

**Question.** Should Determ draw a fresh random K-of-K co-creator committee for every block instead of retaining the epoch-seeded committee for `epoch_blocks` heights, with the objective of better availability and resistance to targeted DDoS, without weakening the security requirements — and can the epoch *checkpoint* (pool / keys / regions / randomness history for historical and cross-shard verification) be retained while the *selection* changes?

## 0. Verdict

**Not justified for the stated objective. Conditionally beneficial for a different one.**

1. **Availability under targeted DDoS does not move.** The chain's per-round fault tolerance is at most **one silent committee member** (§4.3, T-A). Two committee members silent in the same round make the round un-abortable — no claim bucket against a silent member can reach the quorum `max(2, K−1)` with only `K−2` live claimers — and the S-050 stall valve then re-derives the *identical* committee for as long as the silence lasts. That bound is a property of the abort machinery, not of the selection policy. Fresh per-block selection changes only *what the attacker knows*: under the epoch seed an attacker who silences any two of the K known members halts the chain immediately; under per-block selection an attacker who silences any two of the N pool members halts it after an expected `N(N−1) / (K(K−1))` blocks — 2 blocks at a pool of 4 with K=3, 2.1 at 7/5, 44 at 30/5 (§4.6, Table 2; `N` is the eligible pool at the head, not the profile's inert `m_creators`). Once stalled, the height — and with it the seed — is frozen in both designs. The recovery a DDoS-resistant chain needs is a *stall re-selection or a lower abort quorum*; both are separate mechanisms with their own (refuted or owner-gated) design history (§4.8).
2. **What per-block selection does buy** (real, measurable, not the stated objective): (a) the committee for height H+1 becomes computable only when the K-th Phase-2 reveal of height H is on the wire — every mesh peer, attacker included, learns it at that moment (§4.4) — i.e. block-assembly-plus-propagation time ahead (milliseconds to tens of ms) instead of an epoch ahead (`epoch_blocks` × block time; 1000 blocks by default), which defeats *adaptive* targeting of a specific committee; (b) a validator's duty, subsidy and fee income are spread over the pool per block instead of concentrating on one K-subset for 1000 blocks (`chain.cpp` creator payout goes to `b.creators`); (c) a transaction censored by a colluding K-committee waits one block for a fresh draw instead of an epoch. None of these is availability.
3. **Where it is free and where it is not.** On chains whose seed is their own `cumulative_rand` (`SINGLE`; `BEACON` with `shard_count == 1`, i.e. the `cluster` profile; and a `SHARD`-role chain that is **not** receiving beacon headers) per-block selection is *already expressible as the genesis parameter `epoch_blocks = 1`* — the producer, the validator and the abort-certificate reconstruction all read `chain.at(H−1).cumulative_rand` through the same two resolvers (§2.3), and the live five-node gate `tools/test_weak_3node.sh` runs exactly that (`regional_test`, N=5, K=3, `epoch_blocks: 1`) and asserts per-block rotation. No code changes, no new accept rule. On a `SHARD`-role chain peered with its beacon the seed is the **beacon's** rand at the shard-epoch anchor (`node.cpp:1343-1347`), the beacon verifies every shard tip's committee from that anchor plus the frozen `cc:[E]` pool (`shardtip_verify.cpp:83-138`), and the light auditor re-derives the same from the checkpoint alone (`light/main.cpp:3899-3902`). A per-block shard seed would require the beacon and the auditor to hold the parent tip (whose `prev_hash` link transitively commits the parent's `cumulative_rand`, `block.cpp:666`) and to verify the seed from it (§8.2). That is real work on genesis-frozen surface for a benefit measured in blocks.
4. **The two mechanisms are separable in principle** — the checkpoint is keyed on the epoch index and freezes the pool; the seed is a separate resolver — but on `SHARD` chains the checkpoint is also the *only* thing the cross-shard verifiers have to re-derive a committee from, so the coupling there is load-bearing, not incidental (§2.2, §6.9).
5. **Two pre-existing findings surfaced by the analysis** are recorded for the ledger regardless of this decision (§11): the abort `event_hash` that re-seeds the post-abort committee is a **free, unauthenticated input** (no verifier — validator, beacon, light auditor — recomputes it; `validator.cpp:151/370`, `shardtip_verify.cpp:126-128`, `light/main.cpp:3899-3901`) and the event is assembled by **whichever in-sync node first holds the `K−1` public claims** (`on_abort_claim` has no self-membership gate), so any peer can choose the re-round committee, and a Phase-2 withholder — which pays nothing — can pre-grind its own replacement committee (§5.2); and the two-silent-member permanent stall (§4.3) is not stated anywhere as a liveness bound, though PROTOCOL.md §12.1 already says "one straggler is tolerated".

Recommendation: record the verdict; do not implement per-block selection for DDoS resistance; if the fairness / censorship-latency benefit is wanted on a `SINGLE`/`cluster` deployment, it is a genesis-time choice of `epoch_blocks` (with the caveats in §8.1), not a code change; put the abort-quorum / stall-recovery question and the two ledger findings in front of the owner.

## 2. The two mechanisms as shipped

### 2.1 Selection of the K co-creators for a height (what every node does)

Producer side, `Node::check_if_selected` (`node.cpp:961-1052`): pool = `select_committee_pool(chain_, registry_, cur_epoch, committee_region)` (`:974-975`), minus the domains already aborted at this height (`:999-1005`); `k_use = K`, or `⌈2K/3⌉` in BFT mode when `avail < K ∧ bft_enabled ∧ |current_aborts_| ≥ θ ∧ avail ≥ k_bft` (`:1014-1025`); `rand = epoch_committee_seed(current_epoch_rand(), shard_id)` folded with every abort event hash in the tail (`:1033-1037`); `select_m_creators(rand, |avail|, k_use)` (`:1040`). Verifier side, `BlockValidator::check_creator_selection` (`validator.cpp:85-161`) re-derives exactly that from the block's own `abort_events` and rejects a mismatch; `check_abort_certs` (`:243-373`) re-derives the per-event committee sequence the same way. The BFT proposer is `proposer_idx(seed, aborts, size)` over the same seed with a `"bft-proposer"` domain separator (`producer.cpp:793-806`).

The seed resolver is the only place the *epoch* enters selection:

* producer: `Node::current_epoch_rand()` (`node.cpp:1315-1352`) — `epoch_blocks == 0` ⇒ `head().cumulative_rand`; `SHARD` role with an anchor header available ⇒ **the beacon's** `cumulative_rand` at beacon index `epoch_start−1` (`:1343-1347`); otherwise the local chain's `cumulative_rand` at index `epoch_start−1` (`:1349-1351`).
* verifier: `BlockValidator::resolve_epoch_rand(epoch_start, chain)` (`validator.cpp:1574-1585`) — the external provider (the shard's beacon-header view) when installed, else the same local rule.

`select_m_creators` (`random.cpp:70-98`) is uniform without replacement (rejection sampling for `2K ≤ N`, partial Fisher-Yates otherwise), so the draw is *memoryless*: nothing prevents the same member, or the same K-set, from being drawn twice in a row (Table 1).

### 2.2 The epoch checkpoint (what only multi-shard chains have)

`Chain::apply` folds an `EpochCommitteeCheckpoint {epoch_rand, members}` at every `(b.index + 1) % epoch_blocks_ == 0` **only when `shard_count_ > 1`** (`chain.cpp:1944-1951`), into the `cc:[E]` state-root leaf; `committee_pin_active(chain, E)` is true only for `shard_count > 1 ∧ E ≥ 1 ∧ checkpoint present` (`committee_pool.cpp:7-11`). On a pinned epoch the pool is the frozen member list, region-filtered, in the same domain order the present-head path would produce (`:13-45`); pubkeys and registration are resolved frozen-first (`:47-75`). Consumers: the producer and every verifier gate above; the beacon's shard-tip committee verification (`shardtip_verify.cpp:83-138`, which also takes `epoch_rand` from the checkpoint); the distress-record fold gate (`node.cpp:1307-1309`); the light auditor's witness re-verification (`light/main.cpp:3585-3910`); the 16-epoch ring and DSN1 snapshots.

On `SINGLE`, `CURRENT`-single-shard and `cluster` (BEACON, `shard_count == 1`) chains there is **no checkpoint at all**: the pool is `registry_.eligible_in_region` rebuilt at every apply (`node.cpp:2543`), and `epoch_blocks` governs nothing but the seed cadence (§2.3). So "retain the checkpoint while changing selection" is vacuous there, and load-bearing only on multi-shard chains.

### 2.3 The coupling, stated exactly

One genesis integer, `epoch_blocks` (`genesis.hpp:236`, default 1000, mixed into the genesis hash), sets (i) the checkpoint cadence and (ii) the seed cadence. Separating them means a second period for (ii). But on the chains where (i) does not exist, (ii) alone is already per-block at `epoch_blocks = 1`: with `epoch_index = H`, `epoch_start = H`, both resolvers return `chain.at(H−1).cumulative_rand`, i.e. the previous block's rand — for the producer (`node.cpp:1349-1351`, `epoch_start > height()` is false at the next height) and for the verifier validating `b.index == height()` (`validator.cpp:1581-1584`). In-process harnesses already run this configuration (`src/main.cpp` sets `g.epoch_blocks = 1` in some twenty gates, including `test-register-create-only`), and live multi-node gates run `epoch_blocks` of 1, 2 and 4: `tools/test_weak_3node.sh` (`regional_test` = SHARD + CURRENT, no beacon peer, `epoch_blocks: 1`, asserts per-block rotation), `test_weak_mode.sh` (`epoch_blocks: 2`), `test_extended_epoch_committee.sh` (`web_test` = SHARD + EXTENDED, `epoch_blocks: 4`). None of these is the `cluster` profile. A `SHARD`-role node without beacon headers falls through to its local chain rand (`node.cpp:1343-1345` fails, `:1349-1351`; the validator provider returns `nullopt` on the same test, `:384-394`), so the per-block configuration is valid there and becomes beacon-coupled only once BEACON_HEADERs arrive.

The one genesis-load constraint is `beacon_shard_regions` ⇒ `epoch_blocks ≥ 2` (`genesis.cpp:254-260`): on an EXTENDED beacon, epoch 1's anchor would be the genesis block, which BEACON_HEADER gossip never carries. That constraint is exactly the cross-shard coupling of §2.2 showing through.

## 3. The selection property, defined precisely

**P-fresh (memoryless uniform).** For each height H the committee is a uniformly random K-subset of the eligible pool `N(H)` at that height, independent of the committees at other heights given the seed. This is what `select_m_creators` over a fresh seed gives. Consequences: a member can serve consecutive heights (probability `K/N` per height); the whole committee repeats with probability `1/C(N,K)` (Table 1); at `N(H) = K` the draw is the identity on the set, every height.

**P-turnover (forced partial turnover).** The committee at H must differ from the one at H−1 in at least `t ≥ 1` members. This is a *different distribution*: the draw is conditioned on the previous committee, so it needs the previous committee as an input (it is in the previous block, so verifiable), it is impossible when `N(H) − K < t` (at `N = K` no different membership exists; the rule must degrade to identity), and it *structures* the sequence in the attacker's favour at small margins: at `N = K+1`, `t = 1` means the one idle node is deterministically on the next committee and exactly one incumbent leaves — the attacker knows one member of every future committee in advance. Turnover also shrinks the effective pool per draw from `C(N,K)` to `C(N,K) − C(N−t', ...)` sets, which slightly *increases* the chance that a fixed flooded pair lands together. Nothing below assumes either policy is safe; P-turnover is compared in §7 and rejected on these grounds.

**Which N.** All statements use `N(H) = |eligible_in_region(committee_region)|` at the head (or the frozen `cc:[E]` list on a pinned epoch) minus the height's abort exclusions — the quantity `check_creator_selection` actually draws from. `m_creators` is runtime-inert (DECISION-LOG 2026-08-14) and appears nowhere below.

*Table 1 — repeat probability of the same K-set under P-fresh, `1/C(N,K)`.*

| N / K | 3/3 | 4/3 | 5/4 | 7/5 | 10/5 | 20/5 | 50/5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| repeat | 1 | 0.25 | 0.20 | 0.048 | 0.004 | 6.4e-5 | 4.7e-7 |

## 4. Availability and the DDoS benefit, measured against the shipped fault model

### 4.1 What a round needs and what "unavailable" means here

A round at height H needs, in order: all `K` contribs (Phase 1; the trigger is committee completeness, S-058), then all `K` block signatures in MD mode (`Q = ⌈2k_bft/3⌉` of the `k_bft` committee in BFT mode, `producer.cpp:818-830`). Phase timers are `tx_commit_ms` / `block_sig_ms` (50 ms `cluster`, 200 ms `web`, 300 ms `regional`, 600 ms `global`, 20 ms `tactical`; `params.hpp:209-263`); a healthy round completes on message completeness, not on the timer, so a block takes a few network RTTs. A member is *silent* for the round if its message does not arrive before the timer; silence can be a crash, a saturated link (volumetric DDoS), induced latency above the timer, or a Byzantine withholding — the protocol cannot distinguish these, and neither does this analysis.

### 4.2 The recovery machinery, as shipped

1. **Abort.** On a phase timeout each *committee member* claims against the first missing member in creator order (`node.cpp:1663-1706`, `:1708-1762`); claims are admitted only from committee members and only against committee members (`:1772-1779`); a bucket of `max(2, K−1)` distinct claims (`params.hpp:158`, mirrored at `validator.cpp:340-342` as an exact count) becomes an `AbortEvent` that excludes the named member for the rest of the height and re-seeds the committee (`:1813-1851`, `:1035-1037`). Cost to the excluded member on a Phase-1 abort: `SUSPENSION_SLASH = 10` stake and an exponential suspension `10·2^(count−1)` blocks (`chain.cpp:1783-1800`, `params.hpp:35-37`, `:81`) — and, at the default provisioning (`initial_stake == min_stake == 1000`), ejection from the eligible pool on the **first** abort, since `990 < 1000` (`eligibility_floor.hpp:85`; DECISION-LOG 2026-08-14 "ejection is at the FIRST abort").
2. **BFT escalation.** When the remaining pool cannot form K and `|current_aborts_| ≥ θ` (default `θ = 1`, `genesis.hpp:167-168`), a `⌈2K/3⌉` committee finalizes on `Q` signatures (`node.cpp:1014-1025`, `validator.cpp:124-135`). It is reachable **only through aborts**.
3. **Stall valve.** If no block lands for 5 s with an immobile abort tail (or 30 s regardless), `maybe_stall_reset_locked` clears the abort tail, resets the round and re-runs `check_if_selected` (`node.cpp:1622-1661`). With the tail cleared and the height unchanged, the seed is unchanged, so **the valve re-derives the identical committee** (DECISION-LOG 2026-08-12 "final+8": "a stalled height freezes the entire epoch"). Only committee members arm round timers, so only they run the valve.

### 4.3 The bound that decides the question

**T-A (at most one silent member per round).** At any height, in either selection design, no abort can ever form **against** a silent member when the committee contains `s ≥ 2` silent members; hence `s ≤ 1` is necessary for the round to recover by exclusion.

*Proof.* Claims are admitted only from committee members and only against committee members (`node.cpp:1772-1779`; mirrored `validator.cpp:349-353`), never from the named member, and are bucketed by `(round, missing_creator)` (`node.cpp:1796`). A bucket against a silent member can therefore hold at most `K − s` distinct claims; the quorum is `max(2, K−1)`; `K − s ≥ K − 1 ⇔ s ≤ 1` (at K=2 the quorum 2 exceeds the single claimer even for `s = 1`, the S-044 crash-stop). With `s ≥ 2` no silent member is ever excluded; the valve clears the tail and re-derives the same committee (seed frozen with the height); the straggler path and the S-047 relay only re-deliver stored messages. In MD mode finalization needs all K signatures, so the round cannot complete either. ∎

Two precisions, both from the review. (i) The `⇐` direction is *not* claimed: `s ≤ 1` is necessary, not sufficient — the S-047 wedge (two live members of a 3-committee in different phases, claiming into different `(round, missing)` buckets, `node.cpp:1556-1570`, SECURITY.md S-047) happens at `s ≤ 1`, and it is the S-047 relay plus the S-050 valve that converge it; T-A holds *after* that convergence. (ii) Under bidirectional silence all live members share the same `pending_contribs_` and so the same `find_first_missing`, and their claims land in one bucket; under *asymmetric* floods (§4.6) claims can split, and at K=3 an abort can even form against a **live** member, arming escalation — that does not rescue the silent pair either (escalation needs `avail < K`, which needs prior exclusions, `node.cpp:1018-1021`). In BFT mode a round can also finish on `Q = ⌈2·k_bft/3⌉` signatures without any abort (`producer.cpp:815-829`); that tolerates `k_bft − Q` silent signers, which is 1 at K ∈ {5,7} and 0 at K=3 — the bound is unchanged.

At `s = 1` the recovery sequence is: abort (≈ one timer period + claim propagation), exclusion, re-round with `K` drawn from `N−1` when `N − 1 ≥ K`, or BFT escalation at `N = K` when `K−1 ≥ ⌈2K/3⌉` (true for K ∈ {3,4,5,7}). So one silence is tolerated at every shipped shape and two is a halt at every shipped shape — consistent with PROTOCOL.md §12.1 ("one straggler is tolerated") and with DECISION-LOG 2026-08-14 ("`f = 0` is FALSE").

**T-A is independent of how the committee was chosen.** Per-block selection changes neither the quorum nor the valve.

### 4.4 When the next committee becomes predictable

| design | committee for H known from | horizon for an attacker | who knows it first |
|---|---|---|---|
| epoch seed (shipped, `E = 1000`) | finalization of the anchor block `E·epoch_blocks − 1` (or of the beacon block at that index, for shards) | the whole epoch: `epoch_blocks` × block time — block time is RTT-bound and at most `tx_commit_ms + block_sig_ms`, so at `E = 1000` the horizon is seconds to minutes on a LAN and minutes to tens of minutes on a WAN | the anchor block's committee, at its Phase-2 reveal; the last revealer a message earlier |
| per-block (`cumulative_rand[H−1]`) | the K-th Phase-2 reveal of height H−1 reaching the mesh — `BlockSigMsg` carries `dh_secret` in the clear (`producer.hpp:148`) and is broadcast with no committee filter (`gossip.cpp:332-337`); every other input (`dh_inputs`, `tx_hashes`, `prev_hash`) is already public from Phase 1 — so *any peer* computes `cumulative_rand[H−1]` before the block exists | block assembly (including the `state_root` dry-run, `node.cpp:1528-1537`) + broadcast + validate + apply — milliseconds to tens of ms; the next round starts on apply (`node.cpp:2650`) | the last revealer, one message earlier than everyone else |
| deterministic per-height rotation (§7 C) | the anchor, for every height of the epoch | the whole epoch | as the epoch seed |

Pool changes (registrations activating after their 1..10-block delay, suspensions expiring, deregistrations) shift `select_m_creators` indices and are themselves predictable from chain state, in both designs. The per-block horizon is therefore the *tail* of a round, not a whole round: still far below any volumetric flood's ramp, so the directional conclusion (adaptive targeting of a chosen committee is defeated) stands, but the number is smaller than "one round".

### 4.5 Are validator endpoints discoverable? Yes.

Peering is by configured `bootstrap_peers` / `beacon_peers` / `shard_peers` (`node.hpp:78-87`); there is no peer-exchange message. Every connection begins with an **unauthenticated** HELLO carrying `domain` (`messages.hpp:302-318`; R-3 records the role as unauthenticated). Any party that can open a TCP connection to a validator, or that a validator connects to, learns `IP → domain` for that peer. (The `status` RPC's `next_creators` preview is loopback-only and explicitly approximate, `node.cpp:3507-3511`, `node.hpp:31`; it is not the leak — HELLO is.) The registrant record carries no endpoint, so the chain itself does not leak addresses, but an attacker inside the mesh — the only attacker that can observe rounds at all — maps all `N` domains to endpoints. Unpredictable selection therefore hides *who* to hit, never *where*.

### 4.6 The three attacker classes, quantified

Assumptions (stated, not verified in a live network): the attacker knows all `N` endpoints (§4.5); a flooded node is silent (both directions) for the flood's duration; the attacker's reaction time to a newly revealed committee (observe block, derive committee, retarget traffic, saturate) is `r`; the round time is `τ` (RTT-scale, ≤ timers); flooding capacity is `f` nodes concurrently; network delays are otherwise within the timers.

*Asymmetric floods.* Table 2 assumes a flooded node is silent in both directions. An inbound-only saturation leaves the victim's own messages flowing: its Phase-1 contrib arrives, it never receives the others', so it never signs in Phase 2, and the `K−1` live members form a Phase-2 claim quorum against it — one victim per round, no slash (Phase-2 aborts do not deduct, `chain.cpp:1790`), re-round from `N−1`. With two inbound-flooded members the live `K−2` cannot reach the Phase-2 quorum either, and the victims' own Phase-1 claims land in a different bucket (`(round=1, first-missing-in-their-view)`); at `K = 3` those two victim claims can even reach the quorum of 2 *against a live member* (both see `creators[0]` as missing), excluding the wrong node. So asymmetric floods are somewhat more recoverable than Table 2 and somewhat more chaotic; they do not change the conclusion that the recovery is the abort path, not the seed.

(a) **Temporary outage of one node** (no adversary). Epoch seed: if it is a committee member, one abort per outage longer than the timer, then suspension and — at default provisioning — ejection; if not, nothing. Per-block: it is on duty `K/N` of the heights, with the same per-selection consequence. Per-block selection spreads the exposure over all `N` operators (every validator must be highly available all the time) instead of concentrating it on K per epoch; it neither adds nor removes availability.

(b) **Targeted DDoS.** `f = 1`: both designs keep producing blocks; the victim is excluded, suspended and drained at 10 stake per Phase-1 abort — and at default provisioning ejected at the first one, terminally: the S-051 floor lifts *suspensions* only and still requires `stake ≥ min_stake` (`eligibility_floor.hpp:103-130`, `:116`), so 990 is never re-admitted. Per-block selection only delays that single terminal abort by an expected `N/K` blocks; the outcome is identical. `f ≥ 2`: both designs halt for the flood's duration — the epoch design immediately, per-block after the expected block counts of Table 2, which are single-digit for pools below about ten. Against an adaptive attacker (`r < τ`, i.e. retargeting within a round — not realistic for volumetric floods, whose ramp is seconds), per-block selection removes the ability to choose the two victims; against a blind attacker it does not. The lever that actually decides availability is `f` versus the tolerance `1`, not the seed.

(c) **Byzantine withholding.** One Byzantine committee member withholding is aborted, excluded, slashed and suspended; it re-enters after `10·2^(count−1)` blocks and can repeat, at escalating cost. **Two** colluding members silent in the same round halt the chain permanently, un-attributed and un-slashed (no abort forms, so no record and no deduction). Under the epoch seed they need to share an epoch committee (always, at `N = K`); under per-block selection they wait an expected `N(N−1)/(K(K−1))` blocks to be co-drawn, then the height freezes with them on it. Same outcome.

### 4.7 Why per-block rotation cannot advance a stalled height, and what recovery still depends on

The seed for height H is a function of committed state below H (`cumulative_rand[H−1]` under per-block; the anchor under the epoch seed) plus the abort tail *at* H. A stalled height produces no block, so the seed input does not move; the only within-height re-seed is the abort tail, which needs the quorum of T-A; the valve clears the tail. Therefore recovery from `s ≥ 2` depends, in both designs, on (i) a silent member returning, or (ii) a mechanism that re-seats the committee at an unchanged height. (ii) is the space the log has already explored and closed: a wall-clock / time-bucket term (REFUTED "final+9": no bucket granularity satisfies agreement and rotation simultaneously, and the derivation would depend on the selection output), a gossiped or self-declared round marker (REFUTED, "final"/"final+5": a liveness weapon and a slashing escape hatch), block-hash seeding (doctrine: malleable under a fixed digest). The remaining candidates are accept-rule changes on the abort path — a lower claim quorum, or claims from non-committee pool members — each of which trades attributability and cascade risk (the S-044 reasoning) and is owner-gated. None of them is per-block selection.

### 4.8 Bottom line for the objective

Per-block selection is orthogonal to the fault tolerance that DDoS exploits. It is worth `≈ N(N−1)/(K(K−1))` blocks of delay against a blind two-node flood and it defeats adaptive targeting of a chosen committee. It does not change the one-silent-member bound, the abort-slash drain that lets a one-node flood eject a victim under default provisioning (S-010/S-011 fuse), or the unattributed two-colluder halt.

## 5. Randomness and selection safety

### 5.1 What the per-block seed would be, and who can authenticate it

The only admissible per-block entropy is `cumulative_rand[H−1] = SHA256(cumulative_rand[H−2] ‖ delay_output[H−1])` with `delay_output = SHA256(delay_seed ‖ secrets[0..K−1])`, `delay_seed = SHA256(index ‖ prev_hash ‖ tx_root ‖ dh_inputs)` and `dh_input_i = SHA256(secret_i ‖ pk_i)` committed in Phase 1 (`producer.cpp:782-791`, `:1031-1037`, `:1270-1274`). Every full node re-derives it at validation: `check_creator_dh_secrets` (commit opening), `check_delay`, `check_cumulative_rand` (`validator.cpp:530-560`, `:649-658`); every input is digest-covered or commit-pinned. This is precisely the argument the log made when it *rejected* the block hash as a seed ("final+9" §3): the K secrets of H do not exist when H−1 is minted, so nobody can steer beyond one draw. A per-block seed inherits that property unchanged — it is the same value the epoch seed uses, read at every height instead of at the anchor.

Who authenticates it: full validators (apply path, above); shards ingesting **beacon headers** (`check_header_rand_binding`, `node.cpp:2074`, Q1 2026-08-12); light clients (`verify-rand`: `block_hash[H]` commits `cumulative_rand[H]` and `block[H+1].prev_hash` + committee signatures bind it). **Not** the beacon ingesting **shard tips** — `on_shard_tip` runs the sequential/prev_hash check and the committee-signature core but not the three rand gates (`node.cpp:2109-2180`), and it never needs a shard's rand today because shard committees are derived from the *beacon's* rand. That is the R-2 residual seen from the other side, and it becomes load-bearing the moment a shard seed depends on the shard's own previous block.

### 5.2 Bias channels — unchanged in kind, more frequent in opportunity

* **Selective withholding (last revealer).** The member that receives the other `K−1` reveals first can compute `cumulative_rand[H]` and the committee it implies for H+1 before revealing, and can *reject* that sample by withholding — at the cost of a Phase-2 abort against it (no slash: only Phase-1 aborts deduct, `chain.cpp:1790`; exclusion for the height; the re-round has a fresh draw the withholder does not control). It cannot *choose* the replacement. Under the epoch seed this veto is meaningful once per epoch (at the anchor block) and its committee is the incumbent's; under per-block selection it is available at every height. The budget is the same per veto; the opportunities are `E` times more frequent. Estimate, not measured: with a Phase-2 abort costing only exclusion for that height, a member that wants to avoid being *followed* by a specific committee can veto repeatedly — the abort path bounds this only by suspension of Phase-1 silences, not Phase-2. This is pre-existing and is what S-009 already reasons about; it does not change the K-of-K safety argument, because a veto never seats a chosen set.
* **Abort steering (pre-existing, unrecorded).** The re-round committee after an abort is `select(SHA256(rand ‖ event_hash), N−1, K)`, and `event_hash` is whatever the assembling node put in the event: the validator folds `ae.event_hash` (`validator.cpp:151`, `:370`), so do the beacon (`shardtip_verify.cpp:126-128`) and the light auditor (`light/main.cpp:3899-3901`), and `on_abort_event` adopts it (`node.cpp:1854-1900`) after checking the *claims* — nobody recomputes `compute_abort_hash(round, node, ts, rand)` (`random.cpp:102-110`; its only production caller is the assembler, `node.cpp:1816-1818`), nothing bounds `ae.timestamp`, and `Block::signing_bytes` binds only the hash (`block.cpp:667`). The assembler is not necessarily a committee member: `on_abort_claim` gates the claimer and the named member but not the receiver (`node.cpp:1777-1779`), and `current_creator_domains_` is populated on every in-sync node (`node.cpp:1043-1049`), so **whichever peer first holds the `K−1` public claims** assembles, picks `event_hash`, and gossips (`:1813-1848`). Manufacturing the abort still takes `K−1` claims; choosing the replacement committee among all `C(N−1, K)` sets takes zero seats. Composite that matters *more* under per-block selection: a member that withholds in Phase 2 pays nothing (`chain.cpp:1790` gates both the deduction and the `abort_records_` increment on `round == 1`), knows it will be aborted, can pre-grind `event_hash` during the Phase-2 timer and assemble the abort against itself the instant the last claim lands — a zero-cost, per-block, self-serving committee-steering lever. Bounded by K-of-K (a steered committee still needs every member's signature; with fewer than K colluders in the pool no all-colluder set exists), but it is a free targeting lever — it can seat exactly the honest members the attacker is flooding — and it defeats the "one-step-ahead only" property *within* a height. A second consequence (derived, not reproduced): a peer that re-assembles the same `K−1` claims under a different `event_hash` splits the abort tail across nodes (adoption dedups by exact hash, `node.cpp:1862-1865`; the validator then rejects any block built on the variant tail because the twice-excluded member is no longer in the at-event set), wedging every adopter until the valve fires — the C1 non-determinism of AbortCascadeLiveness.md made deliberate. All of this exists today and is recorded in §11 for the ledger.
* **Grinding.** Phase-1 inputs are commitments over secrets chosen before the other secrets are known; a committee member gains nothing by grinding its own secret (the output is a function of all K). A coalition holding **all** K seats controls `cumulative_rand[H]` outright (they choose all secrets) and hence the next draw — self-perpetuation. Under the model's assumption (fewer than K colluders in the pool) no such committee exists in either design; if the assumption is violated, per-block selection shortens the time to first capture from `≈ C(N,K)/C(c,K)` epochs to that many *blocks* (`c` colluders) — noted as the one place where the cadence makes an out-of-model failure faster.

### 5.3 Domain separation and binding

The shipped seed is `SHA256(rand ‖ "shard-committee" ‖ shard_id)` (`random.cpp:169-175`), consumed by `select_m_creators` and, with the separate `"bft-proposer"` tag, by `proposer_idx`; the subsidy lottery reads `b.cumulative_rand` directly (`chain.cpp:1721-1737`). A per-block seed needs no new tag: `cumulative_rand[H−1]` is height-unique by construction (a hash chain from the genesis rand, which binds `chain_id`), shard-separated by the existing tag, and its consumers are already domain-separated from each other. What must **not** be done: reading `b.timestamp` or any reconciled clock (circular — `check_timestamp` requires `timestamp == reconcile_median_time(creator_proposer_times)`, `validator.cpp:1948-1970`, so the input would depend on the output); reading the block hash (doctrine); reading anything from the beacon-header or shard-tip paths that the receiving side does not re-derive (R-2/R-3 residuals stay as they are — this document changes nothing about them).

## 6. Consensus compatibility (per-block seed = `cumulative_rand[H−1]`, pool unchanged)

Each item states the shipped behaviour, whether a per-block seed touches it, and the proof obligation if it does.

1. **Competing blocks at one height (S-048).** Two K-signed blocks at H arise honestly from the abort-vs-finalize race and are resolved by `resolve_fork` (sig count, then *fewer* abort events, then smaller hash; `chain.cpp:2095-2116`) with a depth-1 reorg (`node.cpp:2481-2482`, `:2656-2750`). Both candidates at H derive their committee from the same seed input (`cumulative_rand[H−1]` of the shared parent) and differ only in the abort tail — unchanged. What changes: the *winner's* `cumulative_rand[H]` seeds H+1, so a reorg at H changes the committee at H+1. Today this happens only when H is an epoch anchor; it would happen at every height. The reorg path already ends in `post_append_bookkeeping_locked` → `reset_round` → `check_if_selected` (`node.cpp:2543-2650`), and a round started on the losing head is rejected by peers on `prev_hash` (`on_contrib`/`on_abort_claim` gates). Obligation O-1: a virtual-time gate that forces the S-048 race at H and asserts every node's committee at H+1 is derived from the `resolve_fork` winner (assert at `check_creator_selection`, not at the producer).
2. **Abort retries within a height.** Unchanged: the tail remix is orthogonal to the seed's source. Obligation O-2: `test-abort-cert-validation` and `test-abort-reselection` re-run under `epoch_blocks = 1` (they already do in-process for several fixtures).
3. **Changing committees across heights.** Round state is keyed by `(block_index, prev_hash, aborts_gen)`; contribs and claims from a member that is not on the new height's committee are dropped by `in_creators`. A member that is *off* the committee at H+1 after being *on* it at H holds no obligation. Unchanged in kind (epoch anchors already do this). Obligation O-3: the S-047 relay and the S-050 valve behave identically when the committee changes every height — the valve re-derives from the same head, so it does; gate under the FA virtual harness.
4. **Quorum intersection / `2K > N(h)` (R-4).** The intersection argument is *within a height*: two K-subsets of `N(h)` intersect iff `2K > N(h)`. Per-block selection makes disjoint committees across *heights* routine, which is irrelevant to attributability, and leaves the within-height race exactly as R-4 records it. No obligation beyond R-4 itself; the analysis in §4.6 assumes `N` values above `2K` for the larger rows, which is the R-4-open regime — those rows are illustrative of the DDoS arithmetic, not a recommendation to run there.
5. **BFT escalation.** Gates on `|current_aborts_|` and pool size at the height; the proposer index reads the same seed (`node.cpp:1354-1366`, `validator.cpp:593-597`). With a per-block seed the proposer rotates per height as well. Obligation O-4: `test-bft-*` gates green under `epoch_blocks = 1`; the validator's proposer check and the producer's use the same resolver (they do: `resolve_epoch_rand` / `current_epoch_rand`).
6. **Delayed messages.** A contrib/sig/claim for height H arriving after H is finalized is rejected on `block_index`/`prev_hash`; one arriving from a node that was on committee H but not H+1 is rejected by `in_creators`. Unchanged.
7. **Epoch boundaries.** On multi-shard chains the `cc:[E]` fold keeps its cadence if the pool period is left at `epoch_blocks`; only the seed resolver would move. The `epoch_blocks ≥ 2` load constraint (`genesis.cpp:254-260`) is about the anchor header and stays. On single-chain deployments there is no boundary.
8. **Restart / replay.** `Chain::load` replays through `apply_transactions` and never calls `validate` (DECISION-LOG 2026-08-13 positives), so committee derivation is live-path only and reads `chain.at(H−1)` after load — available. Round state (secret, `round_seq`-free) is per process. Unchanged. Obligation O-5: `test-chain-save-load` + a live re-selection after reload under `epoch_blocks = 1` (exists in-process for the create-only harness).
9. **Snapshots.** `cc:` checkpoints and `epoch_rand` are in the DSN1 state. A per-block seed reads only the head, which is strictly *less* than the epoch seed needs (`chain.at(epoch_start−1)`, up to `E` blocks back). Incidental observation, **untested hypothesis**: `Chain::height()` is `blocks_.size()` (`chain.hpp:92`) and `Chain::at(i)` indexes positionally (`chain.cpp:68-71`), so a snapshot-bootstrapped node carrying a header tail (`decode_state`, `chain.cpp:3219-3225`) has `height() ≠ head().index + 1`. On such a node the per-block seed is actually the *better-behaved* one (`at(height()−1)` is the tail's last element, i.e. the head), while the epoch seed takes the `epoch_start > chain.height()` branch (`validator.cpp:1581-1582`) and silently returns the head's rand for the wrong epoch; the consumer that breaks outright is `start_contrib_phase`'s `block_index = chain_.height()` (`node.cpp:1066`). Pre-existing, independent of this decision, needs its own gate before being recorded as a finding.
10. **Cross-shard verification.** This is where the designs differ materially. The beacon verifies a shard tip's committee from `cc:[shard_epoch].epoch_rand` (or the anchor block) plus the frozen pool (`shardtip_verify.cpp:83-138`); the light auditor does the same from the checkpoint it pins (`light/main.cpp:3899-3902`). Neither has the shard's `cumulative_rand[H−1]`, and the beacon does not authenticate any shard rand (§5.1). A per-block shard seed therefore requires: the beacon to read the parent tip's `cumulative_rand` from the parent header it already holds (sequential tips are required, `node.cpp:2117-2121`), authenticated transitively through `tip.prev_hash == hash(parent)` (the digest binds `prev_hash`; `signing_bytes` binds `cumulative_rand`, `block.cpp:666`); the witness path to carry the parent header alongside the tip so the auditor can re-derive (a bare "parent rand" field in the beacon-assembled, unsigned `ShardTipRecord` would be beacon-asserted, i.e. the R-2 class — see §8.2); a fail-closed rule for a tip whose parent the beacon never saw (liveness loss on the witness path, never a false accept). Each is an accept-rule or witness-format change on genesis-frozen surface with its own review. Obligation O-6 (only if EXTENDED per-block is ever pursued): the producer's seed and the beacon's/auditor's re-derivation agree on every tip, gated at `verify_shard_tip_committee_sig_root` and at the auditor, with a mutant on the parent-header binding.

**Dependency inventory (every reader of the seed or the pool):** producer `check_if_selected` (`node.cpp:961`), `current_proposer_domain` (`:1354`), `rpc_committee`/status preview (`:3512`, `:3804`); verifier `check_creator_selection` (`validator.cpp:85`), `check_abort_certs` (`:243`), the BFT proposer arm (`:593-597`), `resolve_epoch_rand` (`:1574`), `check_creators_registered`/`check_creator_tx_commitments` (frozen-first key resolution, `:72`, `:173`); beacon `on_shard_tip` → `verify_shard_tip_committee_sig_root` (`node.cpp:2109`, `shardtip_verify.cpp:60`); light auditor witness path (`light/main.cpp:3585-3910`); `committee-at-height` does **not** re-derive selection (it verifies the signed `creators[]` against known keys, `light/main.cpp:8399-8430`), so light block verification is seed-agnostic; the D3.5e distress fold gate (`node.cpp:1307-1309`); `Chain::freeze_epoch_committee` (`chain.cpp:815`) and the `cc:` fold (`:1944-1951`); and the S-051 eligibility floor `eligibility_floor_lifted` (`eligibility_floor.hpp:103-130`), a pool reader on both selection surfaces (`NodeRegistry::build_from_chain`, `freeze_epoch_committee`) that re-admits *suspended* domains when the pool falls short of K and never re-admits an under-staked one.

**Fork-freedom is not established by fresh randomness or by K-of-K alone**, and this document does not claim it. Within a height, uniqueness of the committee given `(seed, pool, abort tail)` plus K-of-K gives "no two blocks without a shared signer" only under `2K > N(h)` (R-4, open); across heights the chain is linear because each block binds `prev_hash`. Per-block selection neither strengthens nor weakens either leg.

## 8. Minimal design, if the fairness / targeting-window benefit is wanted anyway

### 8.1 B-SINGLE — a genesis choice, no code

For `SINGLE` and `BEACON`-with-`shard_count == 1` deployments: set `epoch_blocks = 1` in genesis. Invariants (all already enforced): I-1 the producer's and every verifier's seed for height H is `chain.at(H−1).cumulative_rand` (`node.cpp:1349-1351`; `validator.cpp:1581-1584`); I-2 the pool is `eligible_in_region` at the head, identical on producer and verifier (`node.cpp:975`, `:2543`; `validator.cpp:101` with `registry = build_from_chain(chain, b.index)` at `node.cpp:2511`); I-3 abort re-seeding, escalation, proposer election and the valve are untouched. Caveats to record with the choice: the duty-cycle consequence of §4.6(a); the abort-slash fuse (a single flood on any node can eject it at default provisioning); the `beacon_shard_regions` constraint is irrelevant here. Nothing in this variant changes an accept rule: the `epoch_blocks` value is already genesis-hashed and already read by exactly these resolvers.

### 8.2 B-EXT — sketch only, not recommended

For `SHARD`-role chains: a second genesis integer `selection_period` (default `= epoch_blocks`, i.e. the shipped behaviour) that moves only the seed resolver; the pool and the `cc:` fold stay on `epoch_blocks`. Seed for shard height H with `selection_period = 1`: `SHA256(epoch_committee_seed(beacon_anchor_rand, shard_id) ‖ shard.cumulative_rand[H−1])` — the beacon anchor keeps the cross-shard pool binding, the shard's own previous rand supplies the per-block entropy. Required changes: the two resolvers; the beacon's tip verification to read the parent tip's `cumulative_rand` from the parent header it already holds (sequential tips are required, `node.cpp:2117-2121`), authenticated *transitively* — the tip's digest binds `prev_hash`, and `Block::signing_bytes` binds `cumulative_rand` (`block.cpp:666`), so `hash(parent) == tip.prev_hash` commits the parent's rand under the tip committee's K-of-K signatures; the witness path to carry the **parent header** alongside the tip (`ShardTipRecord` is beacon-assembled and unsigned, `block.hpp:588-602`, `node.cpp:1290-1313`, so a bare "parent rand" field there would be beacon-asserted — exactly the R-2 class — and is rejected by the review); the auditor to consume the parent header; fail-closed handling of parentless tips. Invariants: I-4 every verifier of a shard committee re-derives the seed only from values committed under a committee signature it has verified (the transitive `prev_hash` binding suffices; the three rand gates would additionally prove the rand was *derived* correctly, which the source shard's own validators already enforce); I-5 the `cc:[E]` witness remains sufficient for pool membership and keys, and the parent header is sufficient for the seed. Proof obligations: O-6 plus a light-auditor mutant on the parent-header binding. Verdict on B-EXT: the benefit is Table 2 and the targeting window; the cost is genesis-frozen accept-rule and witness-format changes on the beacon and auditor paths. Not justified now.

> **The bound.** At any height, no abort can form against a silent committee member once two or more members are silent (`abort_claim_quorum = max(2, K−1)`; claims only from and against committee members; the S-050 valve re-derives the identical committee), so at most one silent member per round is recoverable — and that only after S-047/S-050 convergence. Two silent members — two flooded nodes, or two colluders — halt the height for as long as they stay silent, without attribution and without a slash. Per-block fresh selection changes only whether the attacker *chooses* the two (epoch seed: yes, at its leisure for `epoch_blocks` heights) or *waits* for them to be co-drawn (expected `N(N−1)/(K(K−1))` blocks: 2 at 4/3, 2.1 at 7/5, 44 at 30/5). ## 13. Owner follow-up: a dynamic `epoch_blocks` computed from network state "to achieve best possible availability"

Evaluated at design stage against §4 and T-A; nothing implemented.

**What the rule could read.** A consensus-visible cadence must be a pure function of state every verifier holds at the anchor: the eligible pool size `N(h)`, `K`, the abort records and suspension states, block timestamps (committee-chosen inside the ±30 s window), abort counts in recent blocks, heights. It cannot read local liveness observations, RTTs, peer counts, the valve's timers or the wall clock — those differ per node (a fork) and are the refuted time-bucket family.

**What a cadence can change, and what it cannot.** §4 established that the selection cadence moves only the targeting horizon (§4.4) and the duty/reward spread (§4.6a, §7). It does not move: the one-silent-member bound (T-A — the abort quorum and the valve are cadence-independent); the stalled-height freeze (a stalled height commits nothing, so no committed input can change while it is stalled — by definition, not by implementation); the abort-slash fuse (a one-node flood ejects its victim at default provisioning at any cadence); or the two-colluder halt (§4.6c). A rule that shortens the epoch "when the network is under stress" therefore re-seeds nothing at the height that is stuck, and at heights that are not stuck the abort path already re-seeds within the height and the suspension already removes the offending member from the pool for `10·2^(count−1)` blocks — the base committee changes anyway.

**The degenerate optimum.** For the two things a cadence does move, the best value on a local-seed chain is `E = 1` whenever `N(h) > K` (shortest horizon, uniform spread, zero extra cost — §2.3, §8.1), and the cadence is irrelevant at `N(h) = K` (the draw is the identity on the set). So the only rule "calculated upon the network state" that improves anything is `E(h) = 1 if N(h) > K else anything`, which is the constant `1`. On beacon-peered shards the cadence is bounded below by what the beacon and the auditor can re-derive (§6.10, §8.2), and a dynamic rule does not change that cost structure.

**What a dynamic rule would cost.** (i) The formula is itself a genesis-frozen accept rule (`check_creator_selection` must apply it). (ii) Epoch boundaries become a function of history: the `cc:[E]` fold, the beacon's anchor read, the light auditor's checkpoint pin and the 16-epoch ring all index by epoch, so every re-verifier would replay the rule to locate anchors — new surface on exactly the cross-shard path that is hardest to keep coherent. (iii) Any input the committee can influence (block timestamps; whether an abort happens — the S-077 veto) becomes a lever on the cadence, a bias channel that does not exist for a constant. (iv) It changes zero behaviour where it matters (`N = K`) and is dominated by the constant elsewhere — the doctrine's "a feature that changes zero behavior is not built" applies.

**Where adapting to network state is meaningful — and none of it is the seed cadence.** (a) The abort quorum versus the pool margin: when `N(h) > K`, claims from non-committee pool members, or a lower quorum, would raise the one-silent tolerance — an accept-rule change carrying the S-044 cascade/attribution trade-off (DECISION CLOCK R-15). (b) Provisioning: `initial_stake ≥ min_stake + SUSPENSION_SLASH × (aborts tolerated)` so a transient outage does not eject a validator (operational; no code). (c) Pool margin `N ≥ K + 2` so a single silence is recoverable by re-selection rather than BFT escalation (operational). (d) A cost for Phase-2 silence (R-16). Each of these is a decision, not a cadence.

**Verdict: not viable as an availability mechanism; not built.** Choose `epoch_blocks` at genesis (`1` on `SINGLE`/`cluster` if the targeting-window and fairness benefits are wanted; `≥ 2` on EXTENDED beacons by the load constraint; the default otherwise) and put the availability question where the lever is (R-15). Recorded as owner item O-5.
